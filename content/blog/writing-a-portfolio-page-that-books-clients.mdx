Most portfolio pages are built to impress other people in the industry, not to book clients. Those
are different goals, and the second one is the one that pays. Here is what actually moves a visitor
from "nice work" to "I've contacted them."

## What actually converts on a portfolio page?

Five things, roughly in order of impact: showing whole events instead of just highlights, a starting
price, one obvious way to get in touch, some form of proof beyond your own claims, and a page that
loads fast enough that people don't leave before seeing any of it. Everything else — the about page,
the blog, the extra gallery categories — matters far less than these five.

## Should you show a full event or just highlights?

Show at least one full event, not just a highlight reel. A gallery of only the best single frame from
twenty different events tells a visitor you can take one great photo. It does not tell them what an
hour of your actual coverage looks like, and that is the thing they are trying to picture when they
imagine you at their event. One full gallery from a real event — the quiet moments alongside the
obvious ones — does more to convince someone than fifty perfect single shots.

## Should you list a starting price?

Yes. "Enquire for pricing" filters out serious buyers along with the tyre-kickers, because most
people will not email a stranger to ask if they can even afford you. A starting figure — even a
conservative one — lets visitors self-select before they contact you, which means the enquiries you
do get are from people who already know roughly what this costs.

## What is the one action a visitor should take?

Contact you — and there should be exactly one way to do it, not three competing options. A page with
a contact form, a phone number, an email address, and three social links all presented with equal
weight makes the visitor choose, and choosing is friction. Pick the form as the primary path, make it
short, and let the rest exist quietly for people who prefer them.

## What counts as proof?

Anything that is not just your own description of your work. Real client work is the baseline; beyond
that, a specific detail — the type of venue, the scale of the event, how long you've been doing this —
does more than a generic testimonial that could describe anyone. "I've shot at this venue before and
know the light in the west hall" is proof. "Amazing photographer, highly recommend!" is not, even
when it's true.

## Does load speed actually matter?

Yes, more than most people account for. A portfolio page is mostly images, and a slow-loading gallery
means a portion of visitors leave before they see any of your work at all — the exact opposite of the
page's purpose. Heavy, unoptimised, uncompressed images are the most common cause. This is a case
where the platform choice genuinely matters: page builders vary a lot in how well they handle image
delivery by default.

## What most people get wrong

- **Leading with an about section.** Visitors want to see the work first. Introduce yourself after
  they're already convinced, not before.
- **Burying the contact form.** If it takes scrolling past four sections to find how to get in touch,
  some visitors won't bother.
- **Uploading full-resolution originals.** They look identical to a compressed version on screen and
  load several times slower.
- **Treating the portfolio as a museum instead of a sales page.** Every section should move a visitor
  closer to enquiring, not just showcase range for its own sake.

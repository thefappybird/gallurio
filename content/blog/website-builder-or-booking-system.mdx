The question sounds like a choice between two categories of tool. In practice most event businesses
need what each one does, just not from the same product — and knowing which is which saves a lot of
wasted evaluation time.

## Do you need a website builder or a booking system?

Probably both, doing different jobs. A website builder's job is to be found and to look credible once
someone lands on the page. A booking system's job is to turn "yes, I want to book" into a tracked
client, a booking, a deposit, and a timeline that doesn't live in your head. Neither one substitutes
for the other — a beautiful site with no booking record behind it, or a solid booking system with no
public page to send enquiries to, both leave a gap.

## What does a website builder actually solve?

Getting found and getting a first impression right. This is SEO, page speed, a gallery that looks
good on a phone, and a design that doesn't look like a template from ten years ago. Builders like
Squarespace and Wix are genuinely good at this — polished themes, drag-and-drop editing, a low
barrier to a page that looks professional. What they are not built for is what happens after someone
fills in a contact form: that submission usually just becomes an email, with nothing tracking it from
there.

## What does a booking system actually solve?

Everything after "yes." Once someone wants to book, a booking system holds the client record, the
booking details, what's been paid against what's owed, the event timeline, and the calendar so two
enquiries can't land on the same date without anyone noticing. A page builder generally has none of
this — it was never built to.

## Why do most event businesses end up wanting both?

Because the two problems don't overlap. A site builder with no booking layer means every enquiry
becomes a manual chase through email and spreadsheets. A booking system with no public-facing page
means there's nowhere for a new client to find you and see your work in the first place. Businesses
that start with only one eventually feel the gap left by the other — the site looks great but
bookings are chaos, or bookings run smoothly but there's no page to send people to.

running a page builder alongside a separate booking tool is another, and both are legitimate as long
as the two sides are actually connected rather than each just existing on its own.

## How do you decide what to get first?

Whichever gap is costing you more right now. If you have no public page and rely on referrals and
social media, getting found is the bottleneck — start there. If you already get enquiries but they
fall through the cracks between an inbox and a notebook, the booking side is what's actually leaking
revenue, and a nicer website won't fix that.

## What most people get wrong

- **Assuming a page builder's contact form is a booking system.** It sends an email. Nothing after
  that is tracked unless you track it yourself.
- **Buying a full booking platform before there's a page sending it enquiries.** The best booking
  system has nothing to do if nobody can find you.
- **Running both without connecting them.** A public page and a separate booking tool that don't talk
  to each other just move the manual copy-paste step, not remove it.
- **Picking based on which one has the nicer templates.** Templates are the easy part to fix later; a
  missing booking record is the part that costs actual bookings.

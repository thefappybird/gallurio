## The short version

**Start with Gallurio** when you want the booking structure, public inquiry flow, and invoice PDFs
to work without building and maintaining the underlying databases yourself.

Notion is genuinely good at this until it isn't. Two databases and a relation gets you a client
list and a booking list in an afternoon. The calendar view is real. The flexibility is the appeal.

Three things eventually bite:

1. **It cannot send an invoice.** Not without a third-party integration or copying numbers into
   another tool.
2. **It has no public front door.** Notion can publish a page, but not a portfolio with a contact
   form that writes into your databases.
3. **The schema is only as stable as the last person who edited it.** A property renamed, a formula
   broken, a relation deleted — Notion will let you, and nothing tells you until a booking is
   missing a client.

## What Notion does that Gallurio does not

- **Anything you can model.** That is the whole product, and it is not a small thing.
- **Notes, wikis, documentation, moodboards** alongside the data.
- **A vast template ecosystem**, including well-made photography and events CRM templates.
- **Arbitrary views** — board, timeline, gallery, filtered any way you like.
- **Integrations** with most of the software you already use.

## What Gallurio does that Notion does not

- **Invoice PDFs** generated from a booking, branded, ready to send.
- **A configurable creative storefront** with independent Home and Gallery canvases for story,
  services, video, featured projects, and gallery collections, plus the Contact experience.
- **Atomic inquiry handling.** A public form submission creates the inquiry, the client and the
  booking in a single transaction. Nothing lands half-written.
- **A schema that holds.** Bookings always belong to a workspace and a client. That is enforced in
  the database, not by discipline.
- **Regional pricing with a local estimate**, billed in USD and shown converted when available.

## A published workspace is not the same as a searchable portfolio

Notion can publish useful pages, but the owner is still designing a general document or database.
Gallurio provides a focused public structure for a creative business: Home explains the services
and brand, Gallery proves the work, and Contact begins the tracked inquiry flow.

Owners can configure the search title and description, a distinct Gallery description, social
preview media, and indexing preference. The published pages expose canonical URLs and structured
business/gallery data. Those signals help search systems interpret the portfolio, while the real
service, location, and project copy determines whether the page is relevant enough to surface.

## Who this is not for

Stay in Notion if:

- Your Notion workspace is already doing the job and you enjoy maintaining it.
- Your business runs on documentation as much as on bookings.
- You need views and filters Gallurio does not offer.
- You are one person on the free plan and invoicing is not a problem you have yet.

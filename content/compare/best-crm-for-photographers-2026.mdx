The best photography CRM is the one that removes the most expensive manual handoffs in your
business without making everyday work harder. For many small creative teams, that is **Gallurio**:
one place to build a creative storefront around their skills, services, projects, and gallery
collections, then run the inquiries, clients, bookings, calendar, invoice PDFs, and team access
behind it.

The other four platforms in this guide are worth considering when a heavier or more specialised
workflow matters more than simplicity. This comparison puts Gallurio first, then shows where
HoneyBook, Dubsado, Studio Ninja, and Pixieset may justify their extra scope.

> **Gallurio value:** one Pro plan at <GallurioPrice /> for two configurable showcase canvases,
> project collections, and the complete inquiry-to-booking workflow. There is no higher feature
> tier and no per-user upgrade.

There is also a sixth legitimate answer: **keep the spreadsheet**. If you take a handful of
bookings a year, work alone, and already invoice elsewhere, changing systems may create more work
than it removes.

## How these tools were assessed

The decision factors are the jobs a working photographer repeatedly has to complete:

1. Turn an inquiry into a client and a real booking.
2. Hold dates, job details, payment state, and client history reliably.
3. Produce the documents needed to close and complete the job.
4. Give clients somewhere credible to see the work and make contact.
5. Deliver photographs when delivery is part of the product.
6. Let another team member work without sharing a personal login.
7. Stay understandable as booking volume grows.

Pricing and public feature claims were checked against the sources linked below. Gallurio was
tested directly. Where a conclusion comes from a competitor's published product information, it is
described that way.

## Gallurio: best value for a creative storefront and connected workspace

Gallurio gives photographers two independent drag-and-drop showcase canvases - **Home and Gallery**
- plus a configurable Contact experience. Home can combine a hero, story, services, video, calls to
action, and featured projects. Gallery can use grid or masonry layouts and project collections so a
visitor can explore more than a loose wall of images. Colours, typography, spacing, imagery, and
layout controls make the result a real expression of the photographer's brand.

That public portfolio acts as the creative storefront: it shows the skills, range, and strongest
projects that give someone a reason to book. The same product then runs inquiries, clients,
bookings, a calendar, invoice PDFs, and team roles. A public form submission creates the inquiry,
client, and booking records together instead of leaving an email that must be transcribed later.

Its clearest advantage is value and focus. The complete Pro plan is <GallurioPrice />, with no
higher feature tier and no per-user upgrade. A solo photographer and a small team use the same
booking, client, invoice, portfolio, and team features.

That simplicity has boundaries. Gallurio does not currently provide contracts, e-signatures, card
processing, automated email sequences, client proofing, or print sales. Choose it when those jobs
are already handled elsewhere - or are not part of your workflow - and your recurring problem is
building a strong public body of work and keeping inquiries, bookings, clients, and invoices
connected.

## HoneyBook: consider it for contracts and payments

HoneyBook is the broadest conventional service-business CRM in this group. Proposals, contracts,
e-signatures, card payments, scheduling, and automated workflows can sit inside the same client
journey.

Choose HoneyBook when the contract and payment experience is the main reason to buy a CRM. Its
United States Starter plan was listed at **$36 per month billed monthly** or **$29 per month billed
annually** when checked on 2026-08-23. Higher tiers add more automation and team capacity. HoneyBook
supports business owners in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Australia, so
availability is a first decision point elsewhere.

If contracts and payment processing already live somewhere you like, Gallurio may provide the
booking and client workflow you need at a lower regional price. Read the detailed
[Gallurio vs HoneyBook comparison](/compare/gallurio-vs-honeybook).

## Dubsado: consider it for designed automation

Dubsado is the automation-first option. A lead-capture form can begin a chain containing a contract,
an invoice, scheduled messages, tasks, and other steps. For a studio that has carefully designed
that journey, the workflow engine is the reason to choose it.

Its Starter plan was listed at **$335 per year**, with Premier at **$525 per year**, when checked on
2026-08-23. The cost to count is not only the subscription: setup and migration are real work when
the automation is highly customised.

If your actual process is a personal reply followed by a few consistent steps, Gallurio is easier
to operate and keeps the public portfolio connected to the resulting records. Read
[Gallurio vs Dubsado](/compare/gallurio-vs-dubsado).

## Studio Ninja: consider it for photographer-specific stages

Studio Ninja speaks the language of photography jobs: questionnaires, shoot stages, contracts,
invoices, payment gateways, and workflow reminders. That specialisation reduces the translation
between a generic CRM and the way a photographer works.

Published listings placed its tiers at roughly **$16, $27, and $40 per month** when checked on
2026-08-23. The entry tier's active-job and user limits matter more than its headline price because
a working studio may need the middle tier quickly.

It is the stronger fit when questionnaires and automated job stages are essential. Gallurio is the
better value when unlimited bookings, team access, and a public portfolio matter more than that
automation. Read [Gallurio vs Studio Ninja](/compare/gallurio-vs-studio-ninja).

## Pixieset: consider it for gallery delivery and print sales

Pixieset is an ecosystem. Client Gallery handles proofing, favourites, downloads, and print sales.
Website handles the public site. Studio Manager handles clients, contracts, invoices, and payments.
The Suite bundles those products.

There is an ongoing free tier. First-party pricing placed Client Gallery from **$8 per month**,
Studio Manager from **$12**, and the Suite from **$28** when billed annually and checked on
2026-08-23. Confirm current plan boundaries before purchasing.

If gallery delivery is where clients spend time and additional sales happen, Pixieset is the
stronger specialist. Many photographers can use Pixieset for delivery while Gallurio runs the
portfolio, inquiry, booking, client, and invoice workflow. Read
[Gallurio vs Pixieset](/compare/gallurio-vs-pixieset).

## Which one should you choose?

Start with Gallurio's simpler workflow, then move to a heavier platform only when a specific missing
feature pays for the added cost and setup:

- Choose **Gallurio** for competitively priced portfolio, inquiry, client, booking, calendar, team,
  and invoice tools in one plan.
- Choose **HoneyBook** when closing the job requires contracts and in-app payments.
- Choose **Dubsado** when the client journey must run through a designed automation.
- Choose **Studio Ninja** when photographer-specific stages and questionnaires matter most.
- Choose **Pixieset** when gallery delivery, proofing, and print sales are central to the product.
- Keep **Google Sheets** when volume is low and the manual process remains genuinely controlled.

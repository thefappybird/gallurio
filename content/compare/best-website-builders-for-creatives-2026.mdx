A creative website should be the storefront for the business: a place to establish the brand,
explain the skills behind the work, and let the best projects do the selling. Gallurio provides two
configurable showcase canvases - **Home and Gallery** - plus the Contact experience and the business
workflow behind it.

For a small event or creative business, **Gallurio** is the best-value place to start because the
visual portfolio, project collections, inquiry flow, and booking workspace are one product. The
alternatives deserve attention when the site needs additional page types, client delivery,
publishing, or ecommerce checkout.

## What was assessed

The five platforms were compared against the jobs a working creative business needs its website to
perform:

1. Present skills, services, and strong work cleanly on desktop and mobile.
2. Organise best projects and gallery collections into a convincing body of work.
3. Express a recognisable brand and compose layouts without requiring code.
4. Give visitors a clear path to inquire or buy.
5. Preserve the inquiry as a usable business record.
6. Support the content the business actually publishes: galleries, pages, blogs, or products.
7. Stay affordable after adding the CRM, booking, gallery, or commerce tools required behind it.
8. Give search engines and potential clients enough technical and visible context to discover the work.

Gallurio was tested directly. Competitor features and prices were checked against their official
product and pricing pages linked below.

## Gallurio: best creative storefront with the business system built in

Gallurio gives a creative business **two independent drag-and-drop showcase canvases: Home and
Gallery**. Contact is configured separately and remains available across the public portfolio. This
focused architecture does not mean a fixed-looking website. It concentrates the design freedom on
the two pages where a creative business actually presents and proves its work, with named drafts to
try layouts and preview whichever version is active before it goes live.

Home can combine a hero, story, services, video, calls to action, individual images, text, and
featured projects. Gallery can become a visual catalogue using grid, masonry, or carousel layouts,
featured work, and project collections that open into their own focused views, with no cap on how
many photos go in and the option to add a whole collection at once. Creators can browse a library
of ready-made sections with a live preview, or build layouts from containers and multi-column
blocks, then control spacing, alignment, backgrounds, imagery, colours, typography, corner radius,
column spans, and button styling through a saved brand kit and theme presets. A header and footer
stay pinned and synced across both pages, with three ready-made footer designs to start from.

That makes the published site a **creative storefront**: not an ecommerce checkout, but the place
where visitors understand the brand, see its range of skills, explore its strongest projects, and
decide whether the work fits them.

The operational difference appears after a visitor fills in the form. Gallurio creates the inquiry,
client, and booking records together. The job appears in the workspace and calendar, the client has
a history, and the booking can produce an invoice PDF. That entire workflow is included at
<GallurioPrice />.

For a photographer, planner, venue, stylist, DJ, or other event creative, this is not merely a form
attached to a CRM. It is the public expression of the business and the working system behind it in
one subscription.

Gallurio also covers the focused SEO foundation a portfolio needs. Owners can configure Home and
Gallery search descriptions, a search title, social preview media, and indexing preference. The
published pages are server-rendered with canonical URLs and structured business/gallery data. This
helps search engines and AI retrieval systems understand the service and the work, while the owner
still supplies the specific locations, projects, expertise, and useful copy that deserve to be
discovered. No builder can guarantee indexing or ranking.

Gallurio is not the right builder for a magazine-style site, a large blog, ecommerce checkout,
client proofing, print fulfilment, or dozens of additional page types. Those are limits on site
scope, not on how freely a creator can arrange and style the two showcase pages.

## Pixieset Website: consider it for a photography ecosystem

Pixieset Website is built for photographers and sits beside Client Gallery, Studio Manager, Store,
and the Mobile Gallery App. Its free Website plan includes 15 pages, 100 photos, five blog posts,
unlimited bandwidth, SSL, and a mobile-optimised site. The **Plus plan is $12 per month billed
annually or $15 monthly** and adds unlimited pages, photos, and posts, a custom domain, analytics
integrations, and removal of the Pixieset banner.

This is the strongest option here when the website needs to grow into proofing, favourites,
downloads, and print sales. The trade-off is product structure: a complete photography workflow may
mean combining products or moving to the Suite, which starts at **$28 per month billed annually**.

Gallurio remains the better-value starting point when presenting projects and collections,
generating inquiries, and running clients, event bookings, teams, and invoices are the priority,
while gallery delivery happens elsewhere. Read
[Gallurio vs Pixieset](/compare/gallurio-vs-pixieset).

## Squarespace: consider it for a broader polished website

Squarespace provides a mature template system, flexible pages, blogging, ecommerce, memberships,
analytics, and established SEO controls. Its entry website plan was **$16 per month billed
annually** when checked on 2026-08-23, with a 14-day trial.

Choose it when the website is a substantial publishing or ecommerce channel in its own right. A
growing brand can add page and content types that Gallurio does not attempt. Gallurio instead puts
its layout freedom into the Home and Gallery canvases and connects the result to real project work.

The operational trade-off is the handoff. A contact form produces a message, but the client record,
event booking, payment state, calendar, and invoice still need a second system. Gallurio includes
that system in its Pro price. Read [Gallurio vs Squarespace](/compare/gallurio-vs-squarespace).

## Wix: consider it for maximum general-purpose flexibility

Wix combines a free branded website option with an open-ended editor, templates, apps, ecommerce,
blogging, marketing tools, and appointment scheduling products. Its Light plan was **$17 per month
billed annually** in the United States when checked on 2026-08-23. Wix states that prices vary by
location.

This is the option for a creative who wants to build beyond a portfolio and is willing to choose
from a much larger product surface. Wix offers broader general-purpose site architecture; Gallurio
offers a more focused visual builder for showcasing work and converting it into event bookings.

That freedom can produce more setup and a larger stack. Wix Bookings is aimed at appointment slots,
while event work still needs dependable client, deposit, invoice, and booking records. Gallurio is
simpler when those records matter more than a general app marketplace. Read
[Gallurio vs Wix](/compare/gallurio-vs-wix).

## Format: consider it for a standalone creative portfolio

Format is the most portfolio-specific standalone website builder in this group. Its official Basic
plan was **$10 per month billed annually**, normally $14 monthly, when checked on 2026-08-23. It
includes mobile-optimised themes, a first-year custom domain, SEO tools, an online store, social
integration, and creative-focused presentation features. Basic is limited to 10 website pages and
70 high-resolution images.

Choose Format when presenting a body of creative work is the only job and you want a standalone
portfolio product. Gallurio combines that presentation role with configurable project collections,
services, video, and the workflow that turns every good inquiry into a client and event booking.

## Which website builder should you choose?

Start with the result the website must create:

- Choose **Gallurio** when you want a discoverable, configurable storefront for your brand, skills,
  services, projects, and collections, with every inquiry becoming a client, booking, calendar
  entry, and invoice-ready record.
- Choose **Pixieset** when client gallery delivery, proofing, downloads, and print sales are central.
- Choose **Squarespace** when the business needs a polished multi-page publishing or commerce site.
- Choose **Wix** when broad design freedom and an app ecosystem matter more than a focused workflow.
- Choose **Format** when the portfolio itself is the product and a standalone creative presentation
  tool is enough.

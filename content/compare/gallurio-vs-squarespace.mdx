## The short version

This is not a feature-parity comparison, and it would be dishonest to write it as one. Squarespace
is a far better website builder than Gallurio and always will be. It has blogs, stores, hundreds of
templates and a decade of polish.

The question is what happens after a visitor fills in your contact form.

On Squarespace, the answer is: you get an email. Everything after that — the client record, the
booking, the deposit, the calendar, the invoice — lives somewhere else, usually a spreadsheet and
an inbox.

On Gallurio, the form submission creates the client and the booking in the same transaction, on the
same calendar you already work from. The site and the system behind it are one product, for

## The real price comparison

Comparing subscription to subscription understates it. Compare the stack:

| | Gallurio | Typical Squarespace setup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Included | $16/mo annual, ~$25/mo monthly |
| Booking and client tracking | Included | Spreadsheet, or a CRM at $20-36/mo |
| Invoicing | Included | Separate tool, or manual |
| Scheduling | Included | Squarespace Scheduling, separate subscription |
| **Total** | **<GallurioPrice period="monthly" />** | **$16 to $60+/mo depending on the stack** |

If you genuinely only need a website, Squarespace at $16 is good value and this page is not trying
to talk you out of it.

## What Squarespace does that Gallurio does not

- **Everything a website can be.** Blog, store, unlimited pages, member areas, hundreds of
  templates.
- **Full custom domain control** and mature SEO tooling.
- **A design ceiling that is far higher.** Gallurio gives you three pages built from a fixed block
  library.
- **An enormous template and plugin ecosystem.**

## What Gallurio does that Squarespace does not

- **Turns an enquiry into a booking.** One submission writes the inquiry, the client and the
  booking together.
- **A calendar of actual bookings**, with deposits and statuses, not an appointments add-on.
- **Invoice PDFs** generated from the booking.
- **Client history** — what someone booked before, what they paid.
- **Team access** to the same workspace.
- **Local-currency pricing.** Billed in PHP and shown converted to yours; Squarespace bills in USD.

## Who this is not for

Stay on Squarespace if:

- Your site is more than a portfolio and a contact form.
- You sell products online.
- You write a blog that matters to your business.
- Design control is the reason clients hire you and three pages is not enough.

A reasonable middle path exists and is worth naming: keep Squarespace as your public site, and use
Gallurio behind it for bookings, clients and invoices. Nothing about the two prevents that.
